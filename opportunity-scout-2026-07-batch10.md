## Survivor — RateBook: the live benchmark for what agencies actually charge — **81/110 · CLEARS**

**What it is:** A contributed-data platform where agencies and the businesses that hire them anonymously submit real engagement terms (scope, deliverables, pricing model, monthly fee) and unlock live market benchmarks in return. Two-sided monetization: agencies pay to price themselves confidently; buyers pay to sanity-check quotes.

**Core value:** Agency pricing is [one of the most opaque areas in business services — five quotes on identical scope vary 300–500%](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/digital-marketing-pricing-2026-agency-costs), and [rates for similar work span 5–10x by tier](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/digital-marketing-pricing-2026-agency-costs). Both sides lose: agencies underprice out of fear, buyers overpay out of ignorance. Every published "guide" is a static snapshot of stale survey data. And there's a genuine why-now: **AI is collapsing the billable hour** — agencies delivering AI-leveraged work can't price by time anymore and are repricing in the dark, which makes live market data suddenly worth paying for.

**The verified gap:** money already moves here — [the 4A's Billing Rate Benchmark Survey analyzed 36,000+ data points across 886 rate cards](https://taskip.net/digital-marketing-agency-pricing-models/) (G1 ✓, agencies buy benchmark access today) — but it's a members-only trade-association PDF aimed at big ad agencies. A direct search for a live contribute-to-unlock platform found **none**. Clutch's directories carry self-reported hourly *tiers*, not deal-level data. The digital/growth agency tail — tens of thousands of shops — prices by rumor.

**Differentiators:** (1) Give-to-get contribution makes acquisition and moat the same motion — the Glassdoor/Levels.fyi mechanic, proven in this engagement's own scoring (RateCard passed the strictest loop gate of all 28 ideas); (2) live and deal-level vs. everyone else's annual PDF averages; (3) the AI-repricing crisis gives the data urgency static surveys can't match; (4) **zero employment-conflict adjacency** — this is pricing data for service businesses, nowhere near networking platforms.

**Gate check:** G1 ✓ benchmark reports are bought today at named prices · G2 ✓ contribution is mechanically required to see data · G3 ✓ 4A's is orthogonal (trade association, enterprise ad agencies); Clutch is a lead-gen directory, different motion · G4 ✓ seed-survey report sells/converts inside 60 days · G5 ✓ no platform dependency.

**Scorecard:** Pain 6 (opacity is chronic, not acute — scored honestly; the AI-repricing crisis is what sharpens it) · Timing 7 (the billable-hour collapse is a 2025–26 phenomenon; benchmark demand rises with pricing confusion) · AI exposure 8 (contributed longitudinal data ≈ AI-proof) · **Loop 8×2** (submit-to-unlock; plus agencies sharing "we're priced at the 60th percentile" as positioning content) · **Saturation 7×2** (static PDFs and directories; no live product found in a direct existence check) · Solo 8 (survey + data product + report engine) · Fit 9 (you've bought, sold, and priced agency work — you know exactly which numbers both sides are guessing at) · Unit econ 6 (agencies $29–99/mo, buyer reports one-off; two-sided ramp is slower than single-sided SaaS) · Defensibility 7 (deal-level longitudinal corpus can't be backfilled; every quarter of data widens the gap).

**Growth loop, explicitly:** Agency 10 submits three engagements to unlock the benchmark, discovers it's underpricing retention work by 40%, raises prices, and cites "the market benchmark" in its next proposal → the *client* receiving that proposal googles the benchmark and lands on the buyer side of the product → meanwhile agency 11, competing for the same client, hits the contribution wall to see where it stands. Both sides of every deal recruit the other.

**The one hard question:** Cold-start credibility — benchmark data is worthless below a critical mass (~100 verified submissions per service category), and agencies won't contribute real numbers to an empty database. The proven answer is the seed-survey: run "The State of Agency Pricing 2026" as a data-PR project first (agencies answer surveys for reports they'd never seed a product with), publish the report, convert respondents into the live product's founding contributors. That's a growth marketer's move, and it's move #1 below.

**Validation (2 weeks, move 1 no-code):** (1) Launch a 12-question anonymous pricing survey targeting growth/digital agencies via communities and newsletters; 75+ completed responses = the mechanic works. (2) Publish the topline findings as a free report; measure agency-side email capture and "notify me when the live benchmark opens" signups. (3) Pre-sell founding contributor seats ($99/yr, data access forever) to survey respondents — 25 sales = build it.

**Revenue + ceiling:** Agencies $29–99/mo; buyer-side quote-check reports $49–199 one-off; annual "State of Agency Pricing" sponsorships. 12-month solo ceiling ≈ **$12–20K MRR**, with the data asset compounding and an obvious expansion path (freelancer rates, fractional exec rates — each a new corpus on the same engine).
