## Idea 1 — RateCard: the Glassdoor of B2B creator sponsorship rates ⚠️ *adjacency flag*

**What it is:** A contributed-data benchmark platform where LinkedIn B2B creators anonymously submit their sponsorship deal terms (rate, format, deliverables, brand size) and unlock market benchmarks in return — plus paid rate intelligence for the brands buying those deals.

**Core value:** Nobody knows what a LinkedIn newsletter sponsorship or Thought Leader Ad collaboration is worth. Creators undercharge or lose deals; brands import broken CPM logic. RateCard replaces DM-polling-your-peers with actual market data — the exact asymmetry Glassdoor and Levels.fyi monetized.

**Differentiators:** (1) Give-to-get contribution model means acquisition *is* the data moat — every new user makes the product better and is recruited by wanting the data; (2) existing players are discovery databases ([Favikon's 10M-profile index](https://archive.com/blog/linkedin-influencer-marketing-platforms), Thinkers360) or workflow tools (Passionfroot) — none has deal-rate data; (3) the audience lives on LinkedIn and shares on LinkedIn, so distribution medium = product subject.

**The signal:** [LinkedIn launched Creator Marketplace inside Campaign Manager in alpha (NA-only, select brands)](https://quasa.io/media/big-day-for-b2b-linkedin-launches-creator-marketplace-and-brandworks); [Top Voices 360 generated $20M+ in year one; Thought Leader Ads get 6x the CTR at 77% lower CPC; LinkedIn newsletters grew 150% YoY](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/linkedin-statistics-2026-b2b-marketing-data); analysts explicitly note [CPM-based pricing on creator deals is a category-wide mistake](https://www.influencers-time.com/linkedin-creator-marketplace-sponsorship-guide-for-b2b-brand/) — pricing chaos is documented, and the market is forming *right now*.

**Scorecard:** Pain 7 · Timing 8 · AI exposure 8 (contributed data ≈ AI-proof) · **Loop 9×2** (submit-to-unlock + creators publishing rate cards on LinkedIn itself) · **Saturation 7×2** (zero deal-rate incumbents; discovery DBs don't compete) · Solo 8 · Fit 10 (you are the ICP *and* the distribution channel) · Unit econ 6 (two-sided monetization ramps slowly; first dollar ~45–60 days) · Defensibility 8 (longitudinal contributed deal data cannot be backfilled). **Weighted: 79/110** — top of batch.

**Growth loop, explicitly:** Creator 10 submits three deals to unlock benchmarks, then posts "I was undercharging 40% — here's my new rate card" to their LinkedIn audience (which is full of other B2B creators) → creator 11 hits the wall, must contribute deals to see the data. Contribution is the signup; the flex-post is the ad.

**The one hard question:** Does LinkedIn's own marketplace publish rate guidance when it exits alpha? (Precedent says no — marketplaces obscure pricing to preserve take rates, which is exactly why Glassdoor coexists with job boards. But watch it.) Second, honest question: does the ⚠️ adjacency to your employer's space make this a non-starter regardless of score — your call, not mine.

**Validation (2 weeks, move 1 no-code):** (1) Typeform survey — 30 B2B creators submit anonymized deal data for a free benchmark PDF; if <15 contribute, the give-to-get mechanic is dead. (2) Publish "B2B Creator Rate Report, July 2026" from that data; measure LinkedIn reach and inbound. (3) Pitch the report's brand-side version to 5 B2B marketing leaders at $199 — tests the money side.

**Revenue + ceiling:** Creators $0 (data contributors) / $19/mo pro tier; brands $99–299/mo rate intelligence + report sales. 12-month solo ceiling ≈ **$12–20K MRR**, with the data asset compounding beyond that.

## Idea 2 — VibeCheck: independent security certification for vibe-coded apps

**What it is:** An automated security scan + AI-triaged report + "security-checked" badge for apps built by non-developers on Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit and the like — sold pre-launch at $149–499, with $29–49/mo monitoring after.

**Core value:** Makers are shipping production apps with catastrophic, *invisible* flaws — [88% of audited vibe-coded apps had Supabase row-level security entirely disabled](https://getcreatr.com/vibe-coding-technical-debt); [Moltbook exposed 1.5M API tokens three days after launch](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-generated-code-vulnerability-surge-2026/). The alternative is a $5–20K pentest (absurd for a $0-revenue app) or a $50–500K rescue later. VibeCheck sells the $299 version of not becoming a headline.

**Differentiators:** (1) Built for *non-developers* — the report explains findings in founder language with copy-paste AI prompts to fix them, where CodeRabbit/Arnica/Escape speak to engineering teams; (2) platform-independent — [Replit shipped a native Security Agent in April 2026](https://vibeappscanner.com/is-lovable-safe) but each platform only checks itself and grades its own homework; (3) the certification badge is the product's marketing: makers launch publicly, and the badge ships with the launch.

**The signal:** [45% of AI-generated code samples introduce OWASP Top 10 vulnerabilities (Veracode, 100+ models)](https://appinventiv.com/blog/vibe-coding-security-risks/); [35 CVEs attributed to AI coding tools in March 2026 alone](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-generated-code-vulnerability-surge-2026/); [~8,000 of 10,000 AI-built startups now need partial rebuild or rescue](https://getcreatr.com/vibe-coding-technical-debt) — prevention demand is empirically funded by fear.

**Scorecard:** Pain 8 · Timing 8 · AI exposure 7 (~2–3 yr clock as platforms absorb basics) · **Loop 7×2** (badge visible at every public launch + a "Vibe App Vulnerability Index" teardown content engine) · **Saturation 6×2** (young but multiplying: native platform scanners, vibeappscanner.com, enterprise tools upstream) · Solo 8 · Fit 7 (maker-community distribution is your game; security depth needs a partner or ruthless scoping) · Unit econ 8 (first dollar <30 days; makers buy impulsively at launch) · Defensibility 5 (scanning commoditizes; brand + vulnerability index are the compounding assets). **Weighted: 77/110**

**Growth loop, explicitly:** Maker 10 launches on Product Hunt/X with the "Security-checked ✓" badge → maker 11, launching next week into an audience that just watched another Moltbook-style meltdown, sees the badge as launch-kit table stakes and buys a scan. Public teardowns of unscanned apps (responsibly disclosed) keep the fear supplied.

**The one hard question:** When Lovable/Bolt ship native "we scanned it" checkmarks (Replit already did), does an independent $299 certification still clear the credibility bar for buyers who can't evaluate security claims at all? The bet: platforms grading their own homework creates the exact trust gap an independent brand fills — but you must build that brand fast.

**Validation:** (1) *(No code)* Manually audit 10 volunteer maker apps using existing scanners + AI triage; charge $99; sell 5 = proceed. (2) Publish "We scanned 25 launched vibe apps: X% exposed user data" — the PR loop's first cycle. (3) Recruit a security engineer as a revenue-share reviewer for report sign-off (keeps solo-operability while buying credibility).

**Revenue + ceiling:** $149–499 one-time scans + $29–49/mo monitoring + agency tier. 12-month solo ceiling ≈ **$15–25K MRR** riding the fear curve.

## Idea 3 — TechCheck: productized due diligence for micro-SaaS acquisitions

**What it is:** A fixed-price ($499–1,500), fixed-format technical + metrics due-diligence report for people buying sub-$200K online businesses on Acquire.com/Flippa — automated code, infrastructure, dependency, and revenue-data analysis with AI triage, delivered in 5 days. Sellers can also buy a pre-listing "verified tech report" to attach to their listing.

**Core value:** Marketplace listings run on [seller-presented data, with severe customer-concentration and key-person risk common](https://blog.acquire.com/how-to-do-technical-due-diligence-on-a-saas-startup/), and the inventory is increasingly AI-built — the same [88%-RLS-disabled codebase risk](https://getcreatr.com/vibe-coding-technical-debt) now hides inside "profitable SaaS for sale" listings. Traditional DD ([$5K–20K pentests, 1–4 weeks](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/saas-due-diligence-checklist-buyers)) doesn't fit a $60K deal. TechCheck is the inspection report for the internet's housing market.

**Differentiators:** (1) Priced to the deal size — no productized player exists below boutique consultancies ([webacquisition.com](https://webacquisition.com/due-diligence/saas-software/micro-saas/), solo consultants); (2) vibe-code-era specialization: explicitly grades AI-generated-code risk, which no legacy DD checklist covers; (3) the seller-side report turns every diligenced listing into an advertisement placed exactly where every buyer browses.

**Scorecard:** Pain 7 · Timing 7 · AI exposure 7 · **Loop 7×2** (seller-side "verified" badge on listings + buyer reports circulating in acquisition communities) · **Saturation 7×2** (boutiques exist; no automated, deal-sized product found) · Solo 7 (~2–3 AI-leveraged hours per report) · Fit 6 (weakest founder-fit of the batch — audience-building in M&A communities is your skill, the domain isn't) · Unit econ 7 (first dollar <30 days; 15–20 reports/mo is a real business) · Defensibility 6 (report corpus becomes proprietary "micro-SaaS risk benchmark" data). **Weighted: 75/110**

**Growth loop, explicitly:** Seller 10 attaches a TechCheck verified report to their Acquire listing and gets more/faster offers → every buyer who browses that listing sees the badge → buyer 11 demands a TechCheck on the *unverified* listing they're eyeing, and seller 12 orders one preemptively. Both sides of the marketplace recruit each other.

**The one hard question:** Does Acquire.com build this in-house as a trust feature (they have every incentive)? Mitigation: multi-marketplace neutrality (Flippa, private deals, brokers) and moving before they do — but this is a genuine sherlock risk from a platform that owns the transaction.

**Validation:** (1) *(No code)* Post in acquisition communities (Acquire buyer Slack, r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, MicroAcquisitions newsletter) offering 5 DD reports at $299 beta pricing; sell 3 = signal. (2) Publish "What we found inside 10 micro-SaaS listings" (anonymized) — the trust-building PR piece. (3) Pitch 5 active sellers on pre-listing reports at $499 — tests the loop-carrying side.

**Revenue + ceiling:** $499–1,500 per report, both sides of the deal. 12-month solo ceiling ≈ **$10–18K MRR equivalent** (report revenue, MRR-ified via a buyer-side subscription for deal-flow scanning).

## Idea 4 — ChannelGuard: YouTube policy-risk audits for creator channels ⚠️ *borderline — did not fully clear the bar*

**What it is:** A scanner + report that grades a YouTube channel's exposure to the platform's "inauthentic content" and AI-disclosure enforcement — which videos look templated/mass-produced to a policy classifier, where disclosure is missing, and what to fix before the next enforcement wave.

**Core value:** [YouTube's January 2026 sweep removed 16 channels with 4.7B lifetime views and ~$10M in annual ad revenue](https://lastplaydistro.com/blog/why-youtube-is-demonetizing-ai-content-in-2026-creators-are-losing-money); [undisclosed synthetic content risks suspension from the Partner Program entirely](https://ytzolo.com/blog/youtube-policy-on-ai-generated-content-disclosure-2026/). Operators of AI-assisted channels have no way to assess their risk except reading tea leaves. ChannelGuard is the smoke detector.

**Differentiators:** Content sites (ytzolo, OutlierKit) explain the policy; nobody productizes the *audit*. VidIQ/TubeBuddy do growth, not policy risk.

**Why it's borderline:** the growth loop is weak (community sharing, no embedded badge mechanic — score 5), the TAM is partly the slop economy YouTube is deliberately shrinking, and policy-classifier heuristics are guesswork against an opaque enforcer. **Weighted: 68/110.** Build only if validation move 1 shows shocking WTP.

**Scorecard:** Pain 7 · Timing 7 · AI exposure 6 · **Loop 5×2** · **Saturation 6×2** · Solo 8 · Fit 7 · Unit econ 7 · Defensibility 4. **= 68/110**

**The one hard question:** You're selling risk prediction against an intentionally opaque enforcement system — when your "low risk" channel gets terminated anyway, the refund demand and reputation damage arrive together.

**Validation:** (1) *(No code)* Offer 10 manual channel-risk audits at $99 in faceless-YouTube communities; sell 5 in a week = revisit the score. (2) Backtest: run your risk heuristics against the 16 terminated channels vs. 16 survivors — publish accuracy. (3) If accuracy is mediocre, kill it and keep the audience.

**Revenue + ceiling:** $99 audits + $19–49/mo monitoring. Ceiling ≈ **$5–12K MRR**, fear-driven and platform-hostage.
