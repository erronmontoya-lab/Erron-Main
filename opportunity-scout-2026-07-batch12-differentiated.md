# Startup Opportunity Scout — July 2026, Batch 12: Differentiated Shapes

Directive: new ideas differentiated from all prior families (no tracking/visibility, no benchmarks/give-to-get, no arbitrage/roll-ups, no conversion tooling, no compliance). This batch deliberately pulled from unexplored shapes: practice/simulation platforms and agentic monitoring. 3 searches (97 cumulative), 2 hypotheses searched + 6 killed in pre-search triage. **Result: 1 clear survivor at 78 — the first survivor from an entirely new family since Batch 8.**

## Survivor — GrowthReps: the daily practice platform for marketers — **78/110 · CLEARS**

**What it is:** LeetCode/Duolingo for growth and marketing judgment. Daily bite-size scenarios — read this A/B test result and call it; allocate this $40K budget across channels; diagnose why this funnel's activation dropped; price this feature — with AI-scored feedback against expert rubrics, streaks, skill graphs, and public profiles. Prosumer subscription ($15–29/mo), team tier later.

**Core value:** Marketing has courses (passive) and jobs (high stakes) and nothing in between — no place to get *reps*. Meanwhile the anxiety driver is at all-time highs: [the value of a marketer in 2026 is shifting from executing repetitive tasks to strategic judgment](https://www.nextnowdigital.com/post/beyond-the-buzzwords-upskilling-for-marketers-in-an-ai-driven-2026), precisely the skill you can't get from watching videos. Deliberate practice with scored feedback is how every other judgment discipline trains (chess, coding, medicine); marketing skipped it because realistic scenario generation and expert-grade scoring used to require a simulation studio. **AI just made both solo-buildable — that's the why-now.**

**The verified gap:** [existing marketing simulations are legacy education products — Markstrat/StratX, Marketplace Simulations, Simbound — sold to universities and corporate L&D](https://www.novela.ltd/post/seven-questions-to-ask-before-you-choose-a-marketing-simulation-in-2026), not prosumer products anyone opens daily. [Reforge is $2,000–3,500 cohort *content* for mid-career strategy](https://careerbldr.com/blog/upskilling-reskilling-guide/); [Exponent is interview question banks for PM/PMM](https://www.tryexponent.com/guides/google-pmm-interview). A direct search for a "LeetCode for marketers" found the phrase used only metaphorically — no product.

**Differentiators:** (1) Practice-first, not content-first — the daily rep is the product, which is a different habit contract than any course; (2) your habit/adoption expertise is the *product DNA*, not the marketing (streak mechanics, difficulty curves, spaced repetition of weak skills — Duolingo's craft applied to professional judgment); (3) a decade of operating experience means you can author and curate the scenario rubrics that make scoring credible — the exact thing an AI-only competitor gets wrong; (4) the scenario corpus + scoring calibration data compound into the moat.

**Platform arc (Shape A → UGC):**
- **Phase 1 — tool:** 150 authored scenarios across 6 growth disciplines, AI-scored, streaks + skill graph. Single-player value from user #1.
- **Phase 2 — platform:** practitioner-contributed scenarios with revenue share (UGC supply side — senior marketers *want* to publish "here's a real decision I faced"), public skill profiles that candidates attach to applications.
- **Phase 3 — optional demand side:** teams/hiring use of profiles and team practice leagues. (Held loosely: the hiring direction is deliberately last and skippable — it drifts toward professional-identity territory that deserves a conflict check at that time.)

**Gate check:** G1 ✓ named prices throughout the analogy set (LeetCode ~$35/mo, Exponent ~$150/yr, Reforge $2K+, junior marketers buy courses constantly) · G2 ✓ streaks/scores/public profiles shared in job hunts — the LeetCode-profile mechanic — plus UGC contributors promoting their own scenarios · G3 ✓ with flag (Reforge and Exponent are adjacent but different motions — content cohorts and interview prep; neither ships daily scored practice; both *could*) · G4 ✓ a $29 "30-scenario sprint" sells via Typeform + Notion + manual AI scoring with zero code · G5 ✓ no platform dependency.

**Scorecard:** Pain 6 (career anxiety is real fuel but prosumer-discretionary — scored honestly) · Timing 8 (AI-displacement anxiety at peak; AI-scoring feasibility is genuinely new) · AI exposure 7 (the scoring engine and corpus compound; raw content is clonable) · **Loop 7×2** (public profiles in job applications + streak-shares + contributor self-promotion) · **Saturation 7×2** (legacy edu-sims ≠ prosumer practice; direct search verified absence) · Solo 7 (AI generates, you curate; scoring rubrics are the ongoing craft) · Fit 10 (habit formation + growth systems + a decade of real scenarios = the founder *is* the product spec) · Unit econ 6 (prosumer pricing, churn-prone segment, volume-dependent; team tier is the margin fix) · Defensibility 6 (corpus + calibration data + status network; LeetCode's moat was community status, which is buildable but slow). **= 78/110**

**The one hard question:** prosumer education churn — motivation decays after week 3 for every learning product ever built, and only obsessive habit engineering (Duolingo's decade of streak science) beats it. That's either the reason this fails or the reason *you specifically* are the right founder for it — it is literally a bet on your stated core skill. Second-order: does scored feedback feel credible to mid-level marketers, or only juniors? (Validation move 2 answers this.)

**Validation (2 weeks, move 1 no-code):** (1) Author 15 scenarios in a Google Doc; score submissions manually with an AI-assisted rubric; sell a "$29 two-week growth sprint" cohort of 20 via marketing communities — completion rate and day-10 retention are the whole ballgame, not signups. (2) Run the same sprint on 5 mid-level marketers you know; ask one question after: "was the feedback credible enough to pay for monthly?" (3) Test the loop: give finishers a shareable skill-scorecard image; measure actual share rate (batch-2's Article4 rule: <20% share = the loop is fiction, re-score).

**Revenue + ceiling:** $15–29/mo prosumer; $49/seat team leagues later; UGC rev-share economics in Phase 2. 12-month solo ceiling ≈ **$10–20K MRR** — slower ramp than B2B, but it's the only idea on the board whose moat *is* the founder's signature skill, and the only one with Duolingo-shaped upside if the habit engine works.

# Board effect

GrowthReps (78) enters as the board's first genuinely new *family* since Batch 8 — and its profile is the mirror image of PromptShare's:

| | **PromptShare** (84) | **GrowthReps** (78) |
|---|---|---|
| Market risk | Low (buyers already pay for adjacent data) | Higher (prosumer discretionary) |
| Execution risk | Moderate (probing infra, platform whims) | Concentrated in one skill: habit engineering — *your* skill |
| Moat | Longitudinal data nobody can backfill | Corpus + status network, slower but Duolingo-shaped |
| Founder as edge | Distribution playbook | Product DNA itself |
| Ceiling | $25K MRR → platform | $20K MRR → consumer-scale upside if habit engine works |

The selection (PromptShare) stands on evidence strength. But GrowthReps is the first idea where the honest answer to "why you and not anyone else?" isn't about distribution tactics — it's that the product *is* your decade of judgment plus your habit craft, and both are unfakeable. If personal conviction and enjoyment weigh into the final call — and for a nights-and-weekends founder they should — this is the one to hold next to PromptShare while you validate.
