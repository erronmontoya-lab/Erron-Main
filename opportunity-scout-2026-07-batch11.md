# Startup Opportunity Scout — July 2026, Batch 11: Platform-Shaped Pulls

Hypotheses now pre-filtered for the confirmed criteria: solo build/validate/scale, platform-shaped (Shape A tool→data-platform or Shape B give-to-get), real durable value, habit surface, no compliance/legal/healthcare, no connection-mechanics, no audience required. 4 searches (94 cumulative), 3 hypotheses tested. **Result: 1 survivor (78) — which turns out to strengthen the already-selected platform rather than compete with it — 1 near-miss (74), 1 dead.**

## Survivor — CreatorCited: AI-answer citation tracking for YouTube creators — **78/110 · CLEARS**
### …and it's really PromptShare's second corpus

**What it is:** Rank/citation tracking for creators: which questions get *your* videos cited in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini answers, which competitors own which intent clusters, and what changed this week. Shareable "Cited by ChatGPT" badges and per-niche most-cited leaderboards.

**Core value:** [YouTube content now appears in roughly 1 in 4 AI chatbot responses, favoring long, specific, authoritative videos](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-creator-news-june-2026) — a discovery channel that operates *entirely outside* YouTube's own search and recommendations, and one [most creators aren't optimizing for at all](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-creator-news-june-2026). Creators already pay named prices for discovery tooling ([VidIQ/TubeBuddy dominate at $10–50/mo](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/tubebuddy-vs-vidiq/)) — but for the *old* channel. Nobody found tracks the new one.

**Gate check:** G1 ✓ (VidIQ/TubeBuddy subscriptions prove discovery WTP; today's workaround is manually asking ChatGPT) · G2 ✓ (citation badges + niche leaderboards — creator flex culture makes this loop *stronger* than the brand-side equivalent) · G3 ✓ (no circling content found from VidIQ/TubeBuddy on AI-citation tracking — checked; they're natural absorbers, not active ones) · G4 ✓ (manual citation reports sell in 30 days) · G5 ✓ (multi-surface probing, no single-platform dependency).

**Scorecard:** Pain 6 (visceral but not yet revenue-linked for creators) · Timing 8 (the 1-in-4 research is fresh; optimization behavior hasn't formed) · AI exposure 7 · **Loop 8×2** (badge flex + most-cited-in-niche leaderboards) · **Saturation 7×2** (zero direct tools found) · Solo 7 · Fit 8 · Unit econ 6 (creator-market pricing: $10–30/mo, volume-dependent, churn-prone) · Defensibility 6 (longitudinal citation data per niche). **= 78**

**The strategic finding — bigger than the idea itself:** this is the *same engine* as PromptShare. Probe prompts at scale → attribute which entity gets surfaced → longitudinal rank data → leaderboard content loop → badge flex. Only the corpus and buyer differ. Which reframes the selected platform:

> **PromptShare is not "a ChatGPT-app tracker." It's an intent-share tracking platform for AI surfaces, with swappable corpora:** Corpus 1 = ChatGPT/Claude apps (builders). Corpus 2 = YouTube citations (creators). Corpus 3 (parked, from Batch 8) = devtool/library recommendations (DevRel teams).

This directly answers the one lingering commercial concern about PromptShare — "is the ChatGPT-app builder TAM big enough yet?" — because the platform's addressable market is now every party that wins or loses when an AI answer surfaces *anything*. Build Corpus 1 first (emptiest field, most acute buyer); Corpus 2 is the expansion that 10x's TAM with ~30% incremental engineering.

**The one hard question (for the corpus, not the platform):** creator WTP is real but thin and churny — Corpus 2 works as an *expansion* on shared infrastructure economics; it would be a grind as a standalone company. Sequencing solves what standalone economics wouldn't.

**Validation:** inherit PromptShare's plan; add one experiment in week 3 — probe 50 "best/how-to" questions in one YouTube-heavy niche, publish the "Most-cited creators in [niche]" leaderboard alongside the app leaderboard, and measure which audience converts harder. Let the data pick which corpus leads.

## Near-miss — InboxLens: the AI-summary simulator for email marketers — **74/110 · one under**

**What it is:** Paste your campaign → see how Apple Intelligence, Gmail's Gemini, and Outlook's AI will summarize it before you send; get a summary-survival score and rewrite suggestions. Shape A: aggregate results become "what survives AI summarization" benchmarks. Per-send usage = weekly habit; founder fit 10 (lifecycle email is your core craft).

**The signal is real:** [Apple's pre-open summaries replace the preheader entirely; Gmail's Gemini rewrites post-open; all three clients key on the first 150–200 characters](https://stripo.email/blog/ai-summaries-in-email-clients-what-gmail-and-apple-mail-changes-mean-for-marketers/) — a mechanical change to email marketing's most-optimized surface, and [Gemini went default-on in Gmail January 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/08/google-adds-gemini-features-to-gmail-message-summaries-proofreading-.html).

**Why it misses:** G3 by the letter — [Litmus is publishing on the exact problem](https://www.litmus.com/blog/ai-generated-summaries), and an AI-summary preview pane is a natural sprint for the incumbent that already owns rendering previews and the email-QA workflow. Saturation capped at 5, defensibility 4 → 74. **Flip condition:** if Litmus/Email on Acid haven't shipped AI-summary previews within two quarters *and* an indie-priced wedge validates (Litmus starts at ~$99/mo — a $19 indie tier is orthogonal), this re-enters at ~78. Watch-listed with a January 2027 recheck.

## Dead — Cross-ESP lifecycle benchmark network

Killed by the free anchor: [Klaviyo publishes deep benchmarks from 183,000 customers at $0](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks), segmented by industry and percentile. A neutral give-to-get network's only edge is cross-platform neutrality, and nobody pays for neutrality when the dominant platform's free data is this rich. G1 fail.
