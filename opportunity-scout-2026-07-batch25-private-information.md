## The terminal finding: the remaining search space is private

135 searches have now exhausted what public information can yield under your constraints, and Batch 21 already explained why: **specialized opportunities are found by insiders, because public search surfaces incumbents, not gaps.** Every idea findable from the outside has been found, scored, or killed. The best idea — if it's better than the 77–81 cluster — lives in exactly one unsearched place: **information only you hold.** Not as a consolation; as the literal next dataset.

Five questions, each of which historically generates category-defining companies, none of which a search engine can answer:

1. **The contrarian belief:** What do you believe about how businesses grow that most growth marketers would disagree with — something you've *seen work* that the playbooks say shouldn't?
2. **The privileged observation:** Running all growth at a 50-person startup, you see dashboards, funnels, and failure modes almost nobody else sees. What breaks every single week that you've never once seen discussed on marketing Twitter? (Stay clear of employer IP and the networking domain — the question is about the *craft*, not the company.)
3. **The recurring purchase that shouldn't exist:** What do you or your team keep paying for — tools, contractors, workarounds — where every renewal makes you think "this is embarrassing that this is the best option"?
4. **The advice you're asked for repeatedly:** What do other founders/marketers ask *you* specifically to help with, more than twice a year? (Repeated unpaid requests are pre-revenue demand data.)
5. **The decade pattern:** Across ten years of brand-building, what's the one mistake you've watched every company make in sequence — the thing you could now predict on day one? A predictable, expensive, universal mistake is a product spec.

Your answers get run through the same gates as everything else — no sentimentality; private information earns no scoring discount. But this is the only dataset with a nonzero chance of containing an 85+ that 135 searches couldn't see, because by definition nobody has published it yet.
